# Punchier boxed subtitles: reference study and edit plan

Research refreshed on 5 November 2026 for the data-centre short. The requested change is stronger subtitles with a background box while preserving the full spoken script and its synchronization. This document extends the [earlier seven-video text study](shorts-text-research.md), rather than treating that previous selective-headline edit as the current brief.

The transferable treatment is a small, immediately readable phrase, strong contrast, emphasis that follows speech, and a clear view of the subject. The box is the requested adaptation for our detailed moving backgrounds. A dark panel should support the words, not become a second oversized title card.

## Evidence and method

The study combines actual-player observations of popular creator Shorts, direct creator interviews, and first-party platform guidance. The earlier study selected seven videos from the official Popular lists of Zack D. Films, MrBeast and Jenny Hoyos. Its recorded view counts establish substantial reach at that inspection; they are not a worldwide ranking or proof that text styling caused the views. This refresh checks four videos from four creators at eleven sampled timestamps and adds sources on readability, timing and evaluation. Three videos overlap the earlier seven-video sample; this is not eleven unique videos. Fresh likes and historical view counts are explicitly distinguished below.

Keep platform closed captions separate from baked-in lettering. A small black subtitle strip can be generated by the player; it is not evidence of a creator's editing style unless it remains when CC is disabled. Approximate positions and sampled timestamps describe observed frames, not an inferred frame-perfect animation curve. Reference footage is studied in its player and is not included in the project.

## What the primary guidance adds

**The opening must be immediately understandable.** In YouTube's January 2025 conversation with Jenny Hoyos and Shorts product lead Todd Sherman, Hoyos emphasizes immediate intrigue and a compact setup and payoff. This is practitioner advice, not a universal experimentally established one-second deadline. Our existing opening already has the contrast between an abstract cloud and a physical neighbour; the subtitles can reveal it in readable chunks. [YouTube creator interview](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)

**Clarity matters more than indiscriminate speed.** In her direct Creator Science interview, Hoyos discusses concise language and readability at 3:32–5:38, acknowledges that readability scores can be distorted by individual words, and explains at 20:52–21:45 why successive important details need room to register. This supports readable chunks and coherent phrase breaks. It does not justify removing words from the approved narration or accelerating every caption. [Creator Science interview and transcript](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)

**Colour and timing are supported controls.** YouTube's own caption feature lets creators correct text and customize fonts and colours. Its timeline controls the start and stop of text clips. We implement those same editing controls against the saved narration word timestamps, so the visible emphasis follows actual speech. [YouTube caption and timeline tools](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en)

**Placement must account for the player.** YouTube's visual guides show where likes, comments, descriptions and screen edges may cover overlays. Keep our caption panel away from the lower and right interface regions, and inspect a phone-sized preview. A fixed project margin is a working choice, not a claim that every device has the same safe area. [YouTube visual guides](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842?hl=en)

**Treat advertising advice as supporting evidence, not a Shorts law.** TikTok recommends vertical composition, UI-safe placement, early hooks and text that provides context. Its performance-ad guidance also offers a fast text-rate recommendation, but an advertising guideline is not evidence that our narration should run that fast. This edit keeps the existing conversational voice and uses the actual word timings. [TikTok creative best practices, updated June 2025](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?redirected=2)

## Applied caption treatment

| Decision | Reason and implementation direction |
|---|---|
| Compact dark background box | Separate the letters from bright sky, building edges and moving people. Use a nearly black or deep navy fill with modest padding and slightly rounded corners. This is the user's requested adaptation; the observed MrBeast/Zack/Jenny samples above mostly use outlined text. |
| Larger bold lettering | The bundled Barlow Condensed ExtraBold is 92 px on the 1080×1920 composition, enabled by shorter phrases. Measure every line including padding and any stroke. Do not shrink one difficult phrase to unreadable size to maintain an arbitrary word count. |
| Short coherent phrases | Aim for roughly two to four words where speech permits. One or two lines are acceptable. Preserve names, contractions, punctuation, numerical ranges and the meaning of qualifications. A name may need a longer two-line phrase. |
| White plus a single accent colour | Keep connecting text white. Use warm yellow to emphasize the current spoken word or selected meaningful terms. The colour must change at the word's native onset and must not invent a stronger claim. |
| Brief arrival accent | Scale from 96% to 100% over four frames, fully visible from its first frame, then settle completely. Avoid a continual shake or repeated bounce that makes the text harder to read. This exact curve is our design choice, not a measured curve copied from the sampled videos. |
| Stable box position | Keep the subtitle panel in the same reading zone between phrases. Check that it does not cover faces, the phone display, water pipes or the meeting table during camera moves. Keep the backing fitted to the phrase rather than stretching across the whole frame. |
| Full verbatim coverage | All 161 script words must still appear once and in order. Maintain the subtitle SRT. Shorter chunks are a layout change, not permission to bring back selective editorial slogans. |

For example, the existing opening can read as `The cloud sounds` → `harmless, right?` → `Until a giant` → `data centre` → `moves next door.` → `Suddenly, it's personal.` Each state uses its own word timestamps, and its last spoken word remains visible long enough to finish reading. Keep `The International Energy Agency` recognizable as a name. The projection still needs its full qualification and dates in the transcript; do not turn it into a claim that household electricity bills double.

The implemented revision has **50 cues containing all 161 script words**, with one or two lines per cue. Very short draft chunks were merged to avoid 0.37- and 0.47-second flashes; the shortest implemented cue lasts 0.6 seconds. Exact final phrase divisions are in `production/data-centres-natural/subtitle-phrases.json`.

The compact box uses opaque `#11151e`, a 16 px corner radius, 30 px horizontal padding, 17 px top padding and 20 px bottom padding, with a subtle 8 px shadow. At the 1080×1920 composition size it sits 350 px above the bottom, inside margins of 70 px left and 150 px right. The measured widest line including padding is 849 px within the 860 px available area. These dimensions are project choices, not dimensions measured from the reference creators.

Text is white, with the current word in `#ffe14a`. The highlight advances at the frame-rounded native word starts and holds until the next word, without making individual words bounce. Every cue is fully visible on its first frame; its whole box grows from 96% to 100% over four frames and then stays steady. The generated SRT retains the exact words and cue timing without visual styling.

The combined 2K export is complete. Its transcript, word timing, audio identity, whole-frame scan and localized roof checks are recorded in the [production verification notes](../production/data-centres-natural/SUBTITLES.md). The [lighting guide](data-centres-lighting-fix.md) contains the reproduced defect and before/after evidence.

## Reproducible editing and review steps

1. Edit phrase/line breaks in `production/data-centres-natural/subtitle-phrases.json`, preserving the exact script from `timeline.json`.
2. Resolve cue starts, ends and any word emphasis from `words.json`. These times already contain the scene's voice offset. Do not add it twice.
3. Run `npm run subtitles:data-centres`, then `npm run subtitles:check` and `npm run typecheck`. Keep the generated JSON and SRT together.
4. Implement the backing, type, emphasis and deterministic entrance in `src/videos/data-centres/Subtitles.tsx`. Calculate animation from the current frame rather than elapsed wall-clock time.
5. Render the opening, longest line, agency name, date range, water/noise scenes and closing question. Inspect cue entrance, steady hold, highlighted word and exit at phone size. Check every line against the available box width.
6. Render the full 1440×2560 video with `npm run render:data-centres:natural`. Verify full transcript coverage and audio identity, then review the moving result, not only selected stills.
7. Run the frame/audio QA helper. Text entrances and colour changes can trigger image-difference flags; classify them against actual subtitle boundaries. Inspect the uncovered world separately so a large caption box cannot hide a lighting defect.
8. Keep before/after evidence, final checks and the current output path in the production notes. Do not describe a full-file brightness check as proof that localized lighting flicker is absent.

## How to judge whether it worked

Before publishing, success means readable exact subtitles, correct speech timing, clear subject visibility and stable lighting. After publication, use stayed-to-watch behaviour and audience-retention changes around the opening, phrase changes and key explanation. Retention spikes can indicate interest or confusion and rewatches, so inspect the content around them. [YouTube retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)

YouTube distinguishes a public start/replay view from an engaged view. Neither the sampled view totals nor likes isolate the contribution of a font, box or colour. This design applies observed editing mechanics; it has not yet been audience-tested. [YouTube explanation of engaged views](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)
