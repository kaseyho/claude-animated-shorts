## Evidence and boundaries

Checked 5 November 2026. The short concerns growing local pushback against rapid data-centre development, not a measured worldwide decline in interest in technology. The animated neighbourhoods and characters are fictional illustrations, not footage of the cited communities.

- [IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI, executive summary, April 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary): central projection of electricity consumption rising from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030. This is a forecast, not an observed doubling. The report also describes growing social-acceptability concerns and explains that electricity-price effects depend on local supply, grid investment and policy. The film does not claim that data centres necessarily increase everyone's bills.
- [Thomson Reuters Foundation, Resistance blooms in Mexico's data centre valley, February 2026](https://www.context.news/ai/long-read/resistance-blooms-in-mexicos-data-centre-valley/): residents in Querétaro seeking accountability and transparency over electricity and water. Resident concerns are not treated as proof that every shortage is caused by a facility.
- [Reuters, Cape Town data-centre objection, May 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/equinix-faces-challenge-to-cape-town-data-centres-over-environmental-concerns-4699126?ampMode=1): community groups sought disclosure of power, water and environmental impacts in a planning application. The film makes no claim about the application's current approval status.
- [AP, Big Tech's fast-expanding plans meet community opposition, January 2026](https://apnews.com/article/21fa7b957664d5dca6788e35ab43b88e): public opposition alongside proponents' arguments about economic benefits and water/power conservation. Only the accessible search excerpt was used; no detailed figures are taken from this article.

Cooling-water use varies with design and location. The film shows resource questions, not inevitable shortages. Investment, revenue and digital services are presented as supporters' arguments, not guaranteed local outcomes. The proposed safeguards are editorial recommendations, not a claim about existing laws.

## Narration and shot plan

The exact narration and measured scene/voice timings are in `timeline.json`.

1. Hook: a resident's phone and a seemingly weightless cloud; a server building rises behind the street.
2. Buildout: new facilities rise in sequence while neighbours react.
3. Demand: server racks and four energy symbols becoming eight, illustrating the forecast doubling.
4. Grid: homes, facilities, power lines and cost symbols share one scene.
5. Water: a cooling tank and pipes; concerned residents, with no empty taps or reservoir claim.
6. Noise: a house beside cooling equipment; stylized sound waves illustrate a concern.
7. Accountability: a diverse group of neighbours gathers to ask questions.
8. Benefits: investment symbols and useful personal technology; mixed resident reactions.
9. Better bargain: residents around a planning table, with water, energy and cost symbols.
10. Callback: the same neighbourhood, the facility and the people all in frame.

No captions, logos or written placards in the picture. All art is procedural and original to the project, using the repository's character-drawing primitives. Narration is synthetic (en-GB-RyanNeural); music and sound effects are synthesized for this short. No reference photographs are included in the output.
